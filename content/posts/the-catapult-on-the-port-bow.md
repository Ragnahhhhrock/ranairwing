HMAS Melbourne had one catapult, on the port bow. The reference photographs show it with a bridle catcher horn, and a jet blast deflector behind it.

## In the sim

- **Position.** Port bow, where the photographs show it.
- **Start.** Press <kbd>R</kbd> and the aircraft appears on the catapult, in the launch configuration: gear down, flaps half, hook up.
- **Run-up.** Hold <kbd>=</kbd> until the engine reaches full power. The launch is refused below about 85%.
- **Launch.** Press <kbd>Space</kbd>. The stroke is under two seconds.

## How the stroke is modelled

The catapult is scripted. It accelerates the aircraft at a constant 30 m/s^2 (about 3 g) along a 45 m run, which takes about 1.7 seconds, and then releases it. It is not a simulation of steam pressure and shuttle. The aim is to keep the catapult and wire mechanics simple and put the effort into the flying.

## The catapult crew

The deck now has crew in coloured jerseys. Two figures in green stand beside the port wingtip, a catapult officer in yellow stands ahead of the aircraft, and a hold-back man in green is by the catapult. They react to what you do. As you run the engine up, the catapult officer gives a turn-up signal. Once power is above about 90% he crouches and touches the deck, the hold-back man gives a thumbs-up, and when you launch the officer points down the track.

The jersey colours follow an approximate colour code, not one checked against RAN practice of the period.

## Tip

Leave the stick alone during the stroke. Then pull back for a second or two to climb away. The full list is in [catapult launch, step by step](/blog/catapult-launch-step-by-step/).
